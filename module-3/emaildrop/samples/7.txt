October Heritage Month Focus and Book of the Month

October is National Disability Awareness Month. When it was first introduced to the calendar in October, 1945, its goal was to help employ disabled veterans. Today, that work continues but now it also highlights the accomplishments in the workplace of persons with disabilities and advocates for equal employment opportunities to all citizens.

At Maplewood Elementary, we will highlight contributions and challenges of people of all abilities. If your family has a connection to this month and something to share, please let us know. The more personal connections and hearing from those with certain life experiences helps our community grow!

 

The Best Day Ever by Marilyn Singer

Our October Book of the Month is The Best Day Ever by Marilyn Singer. The Best Day Ever  is a sweet, playful story about a boy who uses a wheelchair and his exuberant puppy. When the puppy’s boundless energy leads to mischief, the boy must decide how to respond and the two work together to turn a “worst day” back into the best day ever. Full of warmth, humor, and love, this story celebrates the ups and downs of friendship and unconditional care.

The book connects to the Social Justice Standards, Identity by showing that the boy’s disability is just one part of who he is and does not define his abilities, relationships, or sense of self. It affirms that every child has unique experiences and feelings and encourages readers to appreciate the diversity of others’ lives.

Perfect for Disability Awareness Month, The Best Day Ever highlights inclusion, empathy, and respect, encouraging children to see that differences—like using a wheelchair—are part of what makes each person special, while also celebrating the joys and challenges of friendship.

Emergency Readiness Training

Emergency readiness training in schools takes place in September and continues throughout the school year. Training is designed by grade level to ensure that students understand the importance of these drills without causing unnecessary alarm. In accordance with our Trauma Informed Care approach, our drills will not include any props, actors, simulations, or other tactics intended to mimic a school shooting, incident of violence, or other emergency. Our staff review the proper actions in either an evacuation or lockdown drill and discuss any concerns or fears a student may have.

We encourage you to play a role in our trauma informed approach by reviewing the General Response Protocols linked: Emergency Readiness Information and Safe Schools . We encourage to also read our Family GRP Letter

Our first evacuation drill on Tuesday, September 15th went smoothly. See below for the dates of our upcoming drills. Please note, evacuation drills are subject to change as they are weather dependent. If it is raining/snowing on the date of a scheduled drill, we will reschedule it for another day that week.
